Vegetation Changes in Rocky Mountain National Park

Research Question

How has summer vegetation greenness in Rocky Mountain National Park changed between 2001–2003 and 2020–2022?

Background

Rocky Mountain National Park in Colorado contains forests, grasslands, and alpine vegetation. Changes in temperature, drought, and forest disturbances can affect vegetation throughout the park. Studying vegetation over time can help us understand how the environment is changing.

Data and Methods

I used NASA MODIS satellite data to measure vegetation greenness using NDVI. Higher NDVI values generally mean greener vegetation.

I compared July satellite observations from 2001–2003 with observations from 2020–2022. I calculated the average NDVI for each period and subtracted the earlier average from the more recent average to map vegetation change.

Vegetation Change Map

Results

The average NDVI decreased from 0.5452 in 2001–2003 to 0.5298 in 2020–2022, a difference of -0.0154. This shows a small overall decrease in vegetation greenness.

The map shows that some areas experienced larger decreases, while other areas showed little change or became greener. The pink areas represent decreases in NDVI, while the green areas represent increases.

Interpretation

These changes could be related to drought, rising temperatures, wildfires, or forest disturbances. However, NDVI alone cannot tell us exactly what caused the changes. Additional climate and environmental data would help explain these patterns.
